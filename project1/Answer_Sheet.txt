Business Recommendations: 
Implement Region-Specific Weather-Triggered Replenishment:

Instead of a uniform, nationwide inventory policy, create localized inventory triggers based on the weather station closest to each of the 45 stores. For products identified as "hot weather sensitive" (e.g., beverages, sunscreen), set automatic restocking alerts when regional forecasts predict 3+ consecutive days of temperatures above a specific threshold.

Optimize "Pre-Storm" Inventory for High-Volume SKUs:

Analyze the top 3 best-selling products to identify their behavior 1–3 days before a weather event (e.g., heavy snow or rain). For items that spike, such as bread, milk, or snow shovels, increase safety stock levels in the 48 hours leading up to the forecast to avoid stockouts.

Deploy Dynamic Pricing for Perishable Weather Goods:

Utilize the findings to adjust prices on items that have a limited, highly weather-sensitive window. For example, if a cold snap is ending, implement rapid markdowns on winter clothing to free up shelf space for spring items, rather than waiting for a scheduled inventory review.

Tailor Marketing Campaigns by Location Weather Data:

Use weather data to target digital marketing campaigns specifically to regions facing, or about to face, specific conditions. For instance, promote comfort food or indoor products in areas experiencing high precipitation, driving traffic to stores that are experiencing low foot traffic due to weather.

Reallocate Inventory Between Stores Based on Forecasts:

If a severe weather event is predicted, move inventory from a store expected to have low demand to a store likely to experience high demand for a product (e.g., shifting heaters from a warmer, southern region to a colder, northern region).
